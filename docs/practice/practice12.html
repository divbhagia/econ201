Lecture 12: Practice

The markup rule and market power

Lecture 12 slides · Lecture notes (PDF) · Module III: Firms as Price Setters

Work through each problem on paper before you open its Solution.

1. Canyon Kayaks

Canyon Kayaks rents kayaks at a lake. Its demand curve is \(P = 80 - 2Q\), where \(Q\) is kayaks rented per day and \(P\) is the price in dollars. Each rental costs it $20, so \(\text{MC} = 20\). Canyon Kayaks maximizes its profit by renting 15 kayaks a day at $50 each.

(a) Find Canyon Kayaks’ profit margin and its price markup.

Solution

The profit margin is \(P - \text{MC} = 50 - 20 =\) $30 on each rental.

The markup is the profit margin as a share of the price: \[\text{markup} = \frac{P - \text{MC}}{P} = \frac{30}{50} = 0.6\] or 60% of the price.

(b) The slope of the demand curve is \(-2\). Find the price elasticity of demand at 15 kayaks and $50.

Solution

\[\varepsilon = -\frac{P}{Q} \times \frac{1}{\text{slope}} = \frac{50}{15} \times \frac{1}{2} = \frac{5}{3} \approx 1.67\]

(c) Check that Canyon Kayaks follows the markup rule.

Solution

The markup rule says the markup equals \(1/\varepsilon\): \[\frac{1}{\varepsilon} = \frac{3}{5} = 0.6\] which is the 60% markup from part (a).

(d) A second kayak rental opens at the lake. What happens to the elasticity of Canyon Kayaks’ demand, and to its markup?

Solution

Renters now have a close substitute, so more of them switch when Canyon Kayaks raises its price. Its demand becomes more elastic, and by the markup rule its markup falls.

2. Phone cases

A company sells phone cases. Each case costs it $15 to make, so \(\text{MC} = 15\). At the price it chooses, the price elasticity of demand for its cases is 4.

(a) Use the markup rule to find its markup and its price.

Solution

The markup is \(1/\varepsilon = 1/4\), or 25%. Then \[\frac{P - 15}{P} = \frac{1}{4}\] so \(P - 15 = P/4\), which gives \(\tfrac{3}{4}P = 15\) and \(P = 20\).

Check: \((20 - 15)/20 = 5/20 = 1/4\).

(b) A rival brand starts selling very similar cases, and the elasticity rises to 6. Find the new price.

Solution

The markup falls to \(1/6\): \[\frac{P - 15}{P} = \frac{1}{6}\] so \(P - 15 = P/6\), which gives \(\tfrac{5}{6}P = 15\) and \(P = 18\). With a close substitute on the market, the company sets a lower price.

3. Pixel Forge

Pixel Forge makes a video game. Writing and testing the game costs $60 million, whatever the number of players. Each extra download then costs $2 in server fees, so \(\text{MC} = 2\). Games of this kind sell for $5 a download.

(a) Find the average cost per download at 10 million, 20 million, and 60 million downloads. Why does it fall?

Solution

Average cost is total cost divided by downloads, so each download carries its $2 plus a share of the $60 million: \[\text{AC} = \frac{60{,}000{,}000}{Q} + 2\]

At 10 million downloads, \(\text{AC} = 6 + 2 =\) $8. At 20 million, \(\text{AC} = 3 + 2 =\) $5. At 60 million, \(\text{AC} = 1 + 2 =\) $3.

It falls because the $60 million is spread over more and more downloads.

(b) How many downloads does Pixel Forge need to break even at $5? What happens to its profit if it sells fewer, or more?

Solution

Profit is \((P - \text{AC}) \times Q\), so the firm breaks even where \(\text{AC} = P = 5\). From part (a), that is 20 million downloads. With fewer, average cost is above $5 and Pixel Forge loses money on every sale. With more, average cost is below $5 and it makes a profit that grows with every extra download.

(c) At $5, 60 million people buy a game of this kind. Suppose several studios make similar games and split those buyers equally. What is each studio’s profit if there is one studio? Three? Six? How many studios can this market support?

Solution
  • One studio sells 60 million downloads at an average cost of $3, so its profit is $(5 - 3) = $ $120 million.
  • Three studios sell 20 million each, at an average cost of $5, so each just breaks even.
  • Six studios sell 10 million each, at an average cost of $8, so each loses \((5 - 8) \times 10 = -\$30\) million.

The market can support at most three studios. Each one has to pay the whole $60 million but gets only a share of the buyers, so the more studios there are, the higher each one’s average cost. When average cost falls with output, a market ends up with a few large firms.

4. Multiple choice

Q1. (Adapted from CORE Question 7.13) Select all the statements that are correct. A firm’s market power would increase if:

  1. Another firm that sold a close substitute goes out of business.
  2. Buyers in its market start to care more about price than about the features of each product.
  3. The firm gets a patent on its product.
  4. Buyers become more loyal to the firm’s brand.
Solution

Fewer substitutes, a patent, and stronger brand loyalty all make the firm’s demand less elastic, so it can set a higher price without losing its buyers. Buyers who care mainly about price switch easily to rivals, which makes demand more elastic and lowers market power. (a), (c), and (d) are correct.

Q2. A firm maximizes its profit by charging $50. At that price, the price elasticity of demand for its product is 5. What is its marginal cost?

  1. $10
  2. $40
  3. $45
  4. $50
Solution

By the markup rule, the markup is \(1/\varepsilon = 1/5\), so \((50 - \text{MC})/50 = 1/5\). Then \(50 - \text{MC} = 10\), and \(\text{MC} = 40\). (b) is correct.

Q3. Which statement describes a natural monopoly?

  1. A firm that holds a patent on its product.
  2. A firm whose product has no close substitutes because of its brand.
  3. A market where one firm can supply all buyers at a lower average cost than two firms could.
  4. A market where firms agree with each other to charge the same price.
Solution

A natural monopoly comes from the cost structure: with large fixed costs, average cost keeps falling, so one firm serving the whole market has the lowest average cost. A patent and a strong brand are other sources of market power, and firms agreeing on prices form a cartel. (c) is correct.

Q4. A city has hundreds of hair salons. Each one offers a slightly different style and service, and a new salon can open easily. What is the market structure?

  1. Perfect competition
  2. Monopolistic competition
  3. Oligopoly
  4. Monopoly
Solution

There are many firms, entry is easy, and the products are differentiated, so each salon has some control over its price. (b) is correct. In perfect competition the products would be identical.

Q5. (Adapted from CORE Question 7.15) A large retail chain plans to open a superstore in a small town. Select all the statements that are correct.

  1. Local shops argue that many of the chain’s goods are close substitutes for theirs, so the chain faces inelastic demand for those goods.
  2. The chain argues that many of its goods are close substitutes for those in local shops, so demand is elastic and competition will keep prices low.
  3. Local shops argue that once they are driven out, the chain will face less competition and can raise its prices.
  4. The chain argues that local shops sell goods so different from its own that their demand is highly elastic.
Solution

Close substitutes make demand more elastic, not less, so (a) is wrong and (b) is right. If the local shops close, the chain loses its rivals, its demand becomes less elastic, and it can raise prices, so (c) is right. Goods that are very different from the chain’s have few close substitutes, so their demand is less elastic, not more. (b) and (c) are correct.

Q6. A coffee shop starts an advertising campaign that builds loyalty among its customers. According to the markup rule, what happens?

  1. Its demand becomes more elastic, so it lowers its markup.
  2. Its demand becomes less elastic, so it raises its markup.
  3. Its marginal cost falls, so it lowers its price.
  4. Its demand curve is unchanged, because advertising does not affect buyers.
Solution

Loyal customers switch less readily when the price rises, so demand is less elastic. The markup rule, \((P - \text{MC})/P = 1/\varepsilon\), says a lower elasticity means a higher markup. (b) is correct.

Q7. Four companies serve almost all US wireless customers, each sells a somewhat different plan, building a network costs billions, and each company watches what the others charge. What is the market structure?

  1. Perfect competition
  2. Monopolistic competition
  3. Oligopoly
  4. Monopoly
Solution

A few firms, hard entry, and prices that depend on what rivals do are the marks of an oligopoly. Monopolistic competition has many firms and easy entry, and a monopoly has one seller. (c) is correct.

Q8. A city’s water utility is a natural monopoly. Which response is most likely to keep water affordable?

  1. Split the utility into four companies, each with its own pipes.
  2. Leave one utility in place and regulate the price it can charge.
  3. Let the utility set whatever price it chooses.
  4. Require every home to pay the marginal cost of its water and nothing more.
Solution

In a natural monopoly one firm supplies the market at a lower average cost than several could, so splitting it up would raise costs. Left alone, a monopoly sets a high markup. A price equal to marginal cost would not cover the fixed cost of the pipes, so the utility could not survive. Regulating the price of a single utility, or public ownership, is the usual answer. (b) is correct.

Q9. Training an AI chatbot costs a company billions of dollars before anyone uses it. Answering one more question then costs a few cents in computing. Select all the statements that are correct.

  1. The company’s average cost per question falls as more people use the chatbot.
  2. Its average cost is above its marginal cost at every number of questions.
  3. If it charged each user only the computing cost of their questions, it would break even.
  4. A new company with few users would have a higher average cost than an established one with many, so it would struggle to match the established company’s price.
Solution

The training cost is a fixed cost, spread over every question answered, so average cost falls with use and stays above the few cents of marginal cost. Charging only marginal cost would leave nothing to pay for the training, so the company would lose the billions it spent. A newcomer with few users spreads its own training cost over fewer questions, so its average cost is higher and it cannot survive at a price the established company can. This is why markets like this tend to end up with a few large firms. (a), (b), and (d) are correct.

Q10. Which of these are ways a firm can reduce the competition it faces? Select all the statements that are correct.

  1. Buying one of its rivals.
  2. Agreeing with its rivals to raise prices.
  3. Keeping rivals’ products off a platform it controls.
  4. Cutting its price because its marginal cost fell.
Solution

Buying a rival, forming a cartel, and blocking rivals’ access to buyers all reduce competition, and competition policy watches for each of them. Passing a cost saving on to buyers in a lower price does not reduce competition. (a), (b), and (c) are correct.
